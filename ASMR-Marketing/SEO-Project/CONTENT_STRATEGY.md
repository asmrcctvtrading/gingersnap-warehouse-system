#### Core Principle: Coverage vs. Resolution Trade-off (200 words)
- **Coverage:** How much area a camera sees (wide angle camera = more area)
- **Resolution:** How clear the details are (telephoto camera = sharper faces from distance)
- **The problem:** Can't have both in one spot
- **Solution:** Use different cameras in different areas
- **Example:**
  - High-traffic entry = need clear face recognition = use narrow angle
  - Parking lot = need wide coverage = use wide angle

#### RETAIL STORE CAMERA PLACEMENT (600 words)

**1. Front Entrance (Critical Area)**
- **Why:** First point of contact; identity verification
- **Placement:** 
  - Camera facing inbound customers
  - Height: 6-7 feet (captures face at natural angle)
  - Angle: Slight downward tilt to catch faces
  - Distance: Positioned to catch facial details (8-15 feet)
- **Camera type:** 2-4MP fixed lens, narrow angle
- **What to capture:** Full face of anyone entering
- **Common mistake:** Mounting too high (captures top of head)

**Target Length:** 1,500-1,800 words  
**Keyword Focus:** IP camera systems Rizal, CCTV technology, IP vs analog comparison  
**Tone:** Educational but practical; decision-support

**Detailed Outline:**

#### Why This Matters (150 words)
- Story: "A San Mateo business owner spent $15,000 on a system they don't understand"
- Reality: Many businesses don't realize the camera type affects what they can do
- Promise: "By the end, you'll know which technology fits your business"
- Key insight: "It's not about old vs. new—it's about what you need"

#### Camera Types Explained Simply (300 words)

**Analog CCTV (Traditional)**
- **What it is:** Cameras that send video signal down coax cable to a DVR (Digital Video Recorder)
- **How it works:** 
  - Camera captures video
  - Signal travels down cable (limited distance, max ~300 feet)
  - DVR receives signal and saves to hard drive
  - View on TV or monitor at DVR location
- **Picture quality:** Defined in TVL (TV Lines); max 960 TVL (like 0.3MP)
- **Cost:** Low upfront; basic cameras $50-200 each

**IP Cameras (Modern)**
- **What it is:** Cameras that work like small computers; connect to your network
- **How it works:**
  - Camera connects to network (WiFi or ethernet)
  - Sends video over internet protocol
  - Stores on NVR (Network Video Recorder) or cloud
  - View from anywhere (phone, computer, office, home)
- **Picture quality:** Measured in MP (megapixels); 2-8MP typical
- **Cost:** Higher upfront; good cameras $300-800 each

#### Side-by-Side Comparison Table (250 words)

| Feature | Analog CCTV | IP Cameras |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| **Image Quality** | Low (0.3MP equivalent) | High (2-8MP) |
| **Installation Range** | Limited (300 ft cable) | Unlimited (network range) |
| **Remote Access** | Not possible | Possible (phone/web) |
| **Scalability** | Hard to add cameras | Easy to add cameras |
| **Storage** | DVR with limited drives | NVR with large storage |
| **Cost per Camera** | $50-200 | $300-800 |
| **Total System Cost (8 cameras)** | $800-2000 | $3000-8000 |
| **Cable Cost** | Higher (bulky coax) | Lower (standard ethernet) |
| **Power per Camera** | AC power needed | PoE (Power over Ethernet) |
| **Lifespan** | 5-7 years | 7-10 years |
| **Best For** | Budget-conscious, simple needs | Growing businesses, detailed footage |

#### Detailed Comparison: What Each Excels At (700 words)

**Analog CCTV: When It Makes Sense**

*Situation 1: Tiny Budget, Simple Need*
- You need basic surveillance (one or two cameras)
- You have $1,000 total budget
- You don't need remote access
- Example: Small retail counter monitoring

*Situation 2: Existing System*
- You already have analog
- It's working fine
- Upgrade in 5 years
- Don't rip and replace

*Situation 3: Simple, Contained Space*
- Small room (office, small shop)
- All cameras within 300 feet of DVR
- View on monitor in that location
- No need for remote access
- Example: San Mateo office building manager watching hallway

**Why Analog Struggles in Most Rizal Businesses:**
- Can't check footage remotely (owner at home can't see incident that happened)
- Quality too low to identify faces (security incidents become useless)
- Hard to expand (adding cameras = running new cables)
- DVR hard drives fail = all footage lost
- Needs AC power for each camera (electrical work)

**Typical Cost for Analog Expansion:**
- Add 1 camera to 8-camera system: $100-300 for camera + $200-500 for cabling + labor = $300-800 total
- Not practical for business scaling

**IP Cameras: Why Most Growing Businesses Choose This**

*Situation 1: Growing Business*
- You need to add cameras as business grows
- You need detailed footage (clear faces)
- You need remote access (check from home/phone)
- Example: Retail growing from 1 store to 3 stores in Rizal

*Situation 2: Multiple Locations*
- You have one system managing cameras across locations
- All accessible from one dashboard
- Example: Chain of appliance stores in different Rizal municipalities

*Situation 3: Detail Matters*
- Facial recognition is critical

## Part 6: Schema Markup Recommendations for Blog Articles

### Why Schema Matters
Schema markup tells Google what your content is about. For a CCTV company blog:
- Improves local search visibility
- Enables rich snippets (star ratings, images in search results)
- Helps Google understand your B2B expertise
- Can show FAQ snippets in search results

### Schema Types to Implement

#### 1. BlogPosting Schema (Every Article)
Add to every blog post to define it as an article.

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "BlogPosting",
  "headline": "Why Most Rizal Businesses Waste Money on Bad CCTV Systems",
  "description": "5 costly CCTV mistakes businesses in San Mateo make—and how to avoid them. Free assessment.",
  "image": "https://example.com/images/blog-post-1.jpg",
  "datePublished": "2026-11-07T08:00:00+08:00",
  "dateModified": "2026-11-07T08:00:00+08:00",
  "author": {
    "@type": "Organization",
    "name": "ASMR CCTV Trading",
    "url": "https://example.com"
  },
  "publisher": {
    "@type": "Organization",
    "name": "ASMR CCTV Trading",
    "logo": {
      "@type": "ImageObject",
      "url": "https://example.com/logo.jpg",
      "width": 600,
      "height": 60
    }
  },
  "mainEntityOfPage": {
    "@type": "WebPage",
    "@id": "https://example.com/blog/cctv-mistakes"
  }
}
```

**Placement:** Add to the `<head>` section of each blog post page.
